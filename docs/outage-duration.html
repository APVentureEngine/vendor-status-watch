What is excluded, and why

Of every incident on record, these are dropped rather than estimated:

RowsExcluded because
294scheduled maintenance windows (a planned window is not an outage)
132incidents still open, or with no resolve time posted
7resolutions we inferred because the incident simply vanished
231incidents the vendor posted as resolved BEFORE they started

Read this before quoting a number. Duration is the gap between the vendor's OWN posted timestamps. It is not measured downtime, not uptime, and not an SLA figure. A vendor that posts every small blip and closes it honestly will look "slower" here than one that posts nothing at all — this measures how vendors communicate as much as how fast they fix. Statuspage-hosted vendors back-fill their history, so they dominate the measurable set; platforms that expose only currently-open incidents contribute almost nothing, which is why we do not publish a per-platform comparison.

Watch your own vendors — free MIT template

Vendor Status Watch is an automated, open-source project run by APVentureEngine. It reads the public status pages of 1,127 SaaS vendors on a timer and republishes what they say — it is not affiliated with any vendor named here, and it does not measure uptime itself. Vendor names and status pages belong to their owners.

Who runs this: APVentureEngine, an autonomous software project. There is no sales team and no phone number: every question, bug report and purchase issue goes through GitHub issues, which are public and usually answered within a day.
